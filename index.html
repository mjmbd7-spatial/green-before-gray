The idea / 01

A narrow road
can still be green.

The starting point was a memory of a narrow, tree-lined street in Mirpur: mature trees, morning shade, and parrots in the branches.

Here in Khulna, the question is practical: while a neighbourhood is still developing, what can we protect and plan for before the opportunity closes?

This pilot explores satellite greenness around Zero Point–Nirala. It supports an early planning conversation about housing, walking and shared care.

The proposal / 03

Protect. Connect.
Care.

Use the evidence to start a conversation about a better neighbourhood—not to draw a planting plan without checking the street.

01

Keep what
already works.

Identify mature trees and vegetation worth retaining before road and plot decisions are final.

Protect

Confirm species, condition, ownership and construction conflicts. Mark retained trees on the site plan.

02

Make shade
part of the route.

Look for gaps along everyday walks between homes, public transport and neighbourhood services.

Connect

Check utilities, drainage, safe walking space and root room. Low greenness alone does not make land plantable.

03

Give care
an address.

Every proposed planting needs a named steward and a realistic watering and maintenance arrangement.

Care

Agree responsibilities, record survival and replace failed planting. Revisit the same locations each season.

Why this belongs in a housing conversation

A home sits inside a neighbourhood. The proposal treats comfortable walking routes and cared-for shared spaces as part of that neighbourhood’s plan.

What we measured

Sentinel-2 surface reflectance, composited for 15 March–31 May in 2019 and 2026. NDVI uses red and near-infrared reflectance. A threshold of 0.35 defines the vegetation proxy; it is an analyst choice.

Cloud Score+ ≥ 0.60 and scene classification masks were used, with at least three valid observation dates per pixel. Vegetation and matched-period change are recalculated from the exported 10 m rasters into 50 m cells, using exact pixel-overlap area weights. Population retains the original 250 m reporting cells.

What the headline numbers mean

93.42 ha is the geodesic pilot area. Vegetation percentage uses valid pixel area after clipping to the pilot. NDVI change uses the common valid pixel area in both periods. Fine-grid values are recalculated, not copied from the 250 m grid.

Projected cell areas sum to 93.27 ha, about 0.16% below the geodesic area because of geometry/projection handling. The 50 m geometry partitions the projected pilot, excluding a total of less than 1 m² in tiny edge slivers. The older population export is downloadable separately.

What this map cannot tell you

Greenness is not tree canopy, biodiversity, shade quality or land ownership. NDVI changes can reflect vegetation condition, crops, soil and mixed pixels as well as physical land-cover change.

Thermal results did not meet the original coverage rule: no valid temperature pixels remained in the supplied median raster. Temperature, cooling estimates and air-quality claims are omitted.

Historical and population context

Population uses the GHSL 2020 100 m model as supplied in the cell export. Cell totals are approximate and are not summed here into a current pilot population. The source is older and coarser than the optical imagery.

ESA WorldCover 2021 tree and built classes are retained as historical cell context. They are not current tree inventories or proof of a parcel’s availability.
